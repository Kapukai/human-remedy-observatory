# Publication and legal scope

Research reviewed: September 11, 2026. This memorandum describes the scope of this public research repository; it is not a clearance opinion for every future contribution or a prediction of litigation outcomes.

A repository containing accurate, attributed summaries of public judicial decisions, original analytical coding, and reproducible visualizations can serve a legitimate public research purpose. Public availability alone does not settle copyright, privacy, confidentiality, or defamation questions. The publication approach below reduces avoidable exposure while making the research more useful. It does not guarantee that publication is legally risk-free.

## Source material and copyright

Use primary opinions and statutory text, with direct links and precise citations. In *Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020), the Supreme Court applied the government-edicts doctrine to annotations authored by a legislative body in its official capacity. The Court also distinguished independently authored reporter materials, which can receive copyright protection. A court opinion and a commercial website's added headnotes, editorial summaries, illustrations, or database are not interchangeable. Prefer original paraphrases and links; reproduce third-party additions only where permission, an applicable license, or another legal basis supports that use. A repository license covers only material its licensors can license. [Opinion, slip pp. 5–9](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/18-1150_7m58.pdf).

## Attribution, allegations, and analytical labels

Every substantive case record should distinguish **party allegation**, **judicial finding or holding**, **reported implementation**, and **analyst inference**. For example, “the petition alleged false testimony” does not mean that a court found perjury. A procedural loss does not prove the disputed accusation true; a procedural victory does not establish innocence, damages, or completed release.

Calling an accusation “opinion,” “research,” or “system failure” does not automatically protect a false factual implication about an identifiable person. *Milkovich v. Lorain Journal*, 497 U.S. 1, 18–21 (1990), rejected a wholesale opinion exemption from defamation law. Use attributed, narrow descriptions supported by the cited record. Do not infer intentional corruption from an unexplained error, adverse outcome, or statistical pattern. [Opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/1/).

## Personal information and confidential records

Repository policy permits public case captions and the minimum public facts needed to explain a decision. Preserve official pseudonyms and initials. Exclude private attachments, sealed materials, confidential correspondence, personal case dossiers, contact information, identity documents, financial identifiers, and identifying details about minors. Do not reconstruct identities that a court withheld. Public source availability is not a reason to copy every sensitive fact into the dataset.

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 5.2 governs filings made with a court, including specified redactions, exceptions, and protective orders. It is not a GitHub publication safe harbor, and compliance with its basic redaction list does not resolve other confidentiality obligations. Its exemptions—including certain pro se habeas filings—do not authorize republication of sensitive information here. [Rule 5.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_5.2).

## Public and private are analytical categories

The dashboard's sector labels describe the challenged institution or operation; they do not decide legal state action. In *West v. Atkins*, 487 U.S. 42, 54–57 (1988), a contracted prison physician acted under color of state law when performing the delegated treatment function. In *Rendell-Baker v. Kohn*, 457 U.S. 830, 840–843 (1982), substantial public funding and regulation did not make the private school's challenged employment decisions state action. The particular conduct and relationship matter. [West](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/42/), [Rendell-Baker](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/457/830/).

Similarly, identifying a governmental error does not establish an unrestricted damages remedy. Applicable jurisdiction, causes of action, immunity, and remedy-specific limits must be examined. Section 702's sovereign-immunity provision concerns relief other than money damages and preserves other review limitations; the Federal Tort Claims Act has statutory exceptions. These provisions are not interchangeable routes for every public-system failure. [5 U.S.C. §702](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/702), [28 U.S.C. §2680](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2680).

## Research use and court use

The repository is legal and engineering research, not an individualized petition, verified expert opinion, or universally admissible evidence package. Cite the actual legal authorities for their holdings. Case-specific exhibits require their own evidentiary foundation. Authentication does not resolve every admissibility question; a public chart is not automatically judicially noticeable for the truth of its disputed conclusions. [Federal Rule of Evidence 901 and advisory notes](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_901), [Rule 201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre/rule_201).

## Versioning and correction policy

Maintain the opinion date, source URL, access or review date, procedural stage, coding rationale, and known later treatment. Mark unknown implementation as unknown. Describe the collection as selected cases, not a representative sample or causal estimate.

For a substantiated error, correct the record, document the reason in the changelog, regenerate affected charts, and identify the superseded release. For a disputed contribution, mark the uncertainty while checking the primary source. Remove exposed confidential information promptly and assess affected copies; do not preserve it merely to keep an audit trail. Retain a nonsensitive correction note instead. This is a research maintenance commitment, not a claim that every historical judgment is continuously monitored or that all later legal developments have been found.
