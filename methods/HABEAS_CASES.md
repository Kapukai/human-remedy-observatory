# Actual habeas cases involving information, classification and machinery

Research snapshot: 11 September 2026. The expanded catalog identifies **19 actual habeas litigation families**: sixteen targeted additions plus Yick Wo, Hamdi and Boumediene from the original catalog. Every one concerns public custody in this selected set. The proceeding matters: an ordinary criminal appeal, suppression motion, civil damages action and habeas petition are not interchangeable.

The tables distinguish the public power challenged, the information or mechanism at issue, and what the reviewed decision actually supplied. They do not estimate success rates. A favorable review-access ruling, new trial, conditional writ and completed physical release remain separate events. Detailed source notes and limitations are carried in the data records. These are historical primary-opinion summaries with partial source review, not a current-law citator.

## Sixteen added habeas families

### Conviction evidence, science and language

| Case and source | Actual habeas vehicle and public responsibility | Information or equipment status | Reviewed outcome and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mooney v. Holohan (1935)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 110–115](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/294/103/) | Motion for leave to file an original habeas petition in the US Supreme Court, following unsuccessful federal habeas proceedings. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Alleged; no merits fact adjudication in this disposition. | The Court recognized the due-process problem alleged, but denied leave to file without prejudice because available state habeas remedies had not been pursued. Do not count this decision as a granted writ or established factual exoneration. |
| **Pyle v. Kansas (1942)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 214–216](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/317/213/) | Original state habeas petition in the Kansas Supreme Court; US Supreme Court review of its denial. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Allegations supported by submitted exhibits; truth still required determination. | Denial of the habeas petition was reversed and remanded because the supported constitutional allegations had not been determined. The regular appearance of the conviction record did not refute the allegations; their truth was not decided here. |
| **Townsend v. Sain (1963)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 296–300, 320–322](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/372/293/) | Federal habeas petition challenging a state conviction, before AEDPA. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Drug administration documented; disputed coercion and effects required a hearing. | The Court reversed and remanded for a federal evidentiary hearing under its then-governing standards. Keeney later overruled part of Townsend’s hearing framework; modern §2254 restrictions also matter. A drug described as truth serum is not proof that the resulting statement is true. |
| **Miller v. Pate (1967)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 2–7](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/386/1/) | Federal habeas petition challenging a state conviction, before AEDPA. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Independent chemical retesting in habeas established paint; prosecution knowledge was established. | The Supreme Court held the conviction constitutionally invalid, reversed the appellate judgment, and remanded; the district court had ordered release or prompt retrial. The Supreme Court opinion does not document subsequent physical release. This was deliberate evidentiary misrepresentation, not an identified instrument malfunction. |
| **Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes (1992)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 3–5, 11–12](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/504/1/) | Federal habeas petition challenging a state no-contest plea. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Alleged mistranslation; state findings rejected the challenge; further federal fact development was disputed. | The Supreme Court reversed the ruling entitling the prisoner to an evidentiary hearing and remanded under a cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice test. The Court overruled Townsend only in the identified respect. The modern statute imposes additional constraints; do not present the 1992 test alone as a filing guide. |
| **Herrera v. Collins (1993)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 398–405, 417–419](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/506/390/) | Second federal habeas petition challenging a state capital conviction. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Contested new innocence evidence; Court found the showing insufficient. | The Supreme Court held that this freestanding actual-innocence claim did not entitle Herrera to federal habeas relief and affirmed. Do not label these allegations proven corruption, or say the Court held no conceivable innocence claim could ever matter. |
| **Kyles v. Whitley (1995)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 421–422, 434–438, 454](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/419/) | Federal habeas petition challenging a state capital conviction, before AEDPA. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Material suppression established by the Court’s cumulative review. | The Supreme Court reversed denial of habeas relief and held Kyles entitled to a new trial. The judgment does not itself prove physical release. Cumulative materiality is not a rule that every omitted item automatically invalidates a conviction. |
| **House v. Bell (2006)**. [Primary opinion; 547 U.S. pp. 537–555](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/04-8990.ZO.html) | Federal habeas petition; actual-innocence gateway through state procedural default. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | DNA source correction established; the full forensic and witness record was assessed together, not as conclusive exoneration. | House satisfied the demanding innocence gateway and could pursue his defaulted constitutional claims on remand. The Court expressly distinguished serious doubt sufficient for the gateway from conclusive exoneration and hypothetical freestanding innocence relief. |
| **McDaniel v. Brown (2010)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 128–136](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/558/120/) | Federal §2254 habeas petition challenging a state rape conviction. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | The State acknowledged the prosecutor’s fallacy; the DNA match itself was not disproved. | The Court reversed the appellate grant on the insufficiency theory. A newly recast DNA due-process claim was forfeited; other ineffective-assistance grounds remained for further consideration. An insufficiency analysis considers all trial-admitted evidence; it cannot simply delete disputed evidence using a later expert report. The case was decided January 11, 2010, despite one publisher’s 2009 header. |
| **McQuiggin v. Perkins (2013)**. [Primary opinion; pp. 386–387, 392–401](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/569/383/) | Federal §2254 habeas petition; proposed innocence gateway through §2244(d) limitations. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | Innocence not established by the Supreme Court; the district court had found the showing inadequate. | The Court recognized an innocence gateway through the limitations period, but vacated the favorable appellate judgment and remanded, emphasizing the demanding showing and relevance of delay. This was neither proof of innocence nor an automatic exemption from habeas deadlines; second-or-successive petitions present separate restrictions. |
| **Han Tak Lee v. Superintendent Houtzdale SCI (2015)**. [Primary opinion; 798 F.3d pp. 161–167; slip pp. 6, 12 n.5, 14–19](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/143876p.pdf) | Federal §2254 habeas petition; Third Circuit appeal from conditional relief. **Public state conviction and custodian.** | The Commonwealth conceded the scientific foundations were invalid or unreliable; remaining evidence did not supply the necessary support. | The Third Circuit affirmed conditional habeas relief requiring retrial or release within 120 days under the district court order. Review without AEDPA deference rested on this case’s procedural history; the opinion flags later law that might have changed that analysis. No computer breakdown was found. |

## Three close comparators that were not habeas proceedings

These help explain computer and identity errors without mislabeling the procedural vehicle. They are discussed here as comparators and are not additional records in the 63-record catalog.

| Decision | Relevant error and public process | Actual vehicle and outcome |
|---|---|---|
| [Arizona v. Evans, 514 U.S. 1 (1995)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/514/1/) | A recalled warrant remained in computerized court records through a clerical failure. | Review of a criminal suppression ruling. The Court rejected the challenged exclusionary-rule remedy for the court-employee error; this was not habeas. |
| [Herring v. United States, 555 U.S. 135 (2009)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/555/135/) | A police database incorrectly reported an outstanding warrant. | Direct criminal review. ## Separating machine behavior from the ground of relief

The sixteen added habeas records identify no computer malfunction as the ground on which relief was granted. Francis supplies an established equipment-failure example, and the petitioner lost the reviewed challenge. Forensic interpretation, mislabeled samples, omitted evidence, disputed status and untested allegations are different mechanisms; grouping them under “data” does not make them equivalent or necessarily accidental.

Han Tak Lee requires special care. The 2015 opinion also addressed electronic filing and receipt-date issues, preserving the **state's** appeal. The habeas merits concerned unreliable forensic evidence. A discussion of automated clerk behavior therefore should not be recast as a ruling that a computer bug caused the conviction. [Han Tak Lee opinion, jurisdiction discussion and merits](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/143876p.pdf).

The most useful analytical sequence is to distinguish the information's status, the applicable legal duty, the material effect on the judgment, the available procedural route, the relief ordered and what later happened. That sequence describes legal and engineering questions to investigate; it does not create a universal entitlement to a writ whenever an error is reported.

Historical pre-AEDPA decisions do not supply a complete modern filing route. State-conviction habeas currently requires attention to the applicable custody, exhaustion, deference, factual-development, limitations and successive-petition rules. Immigration and military detention raise different jurisdictional and substantive issues. [28 U.S.C. §2254](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2254), [28 U.S.C. §2244](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2244).
